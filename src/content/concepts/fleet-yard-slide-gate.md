## The brief we set ourselves

A yard entrance for trucks and trailers. The gate cycles all day, every working day. It has to keep the yard secure after hours, survive the occasional bump, and need as little attention as possible.

## The design response

**Cantilever slide.** At twenty-four feet, a pair of swing leaves would be long, heavy and slow, and would sweep a huge area of yard. A slide takes none of it.

**Close pickets.** Tight vertical bars are hard to climb and keep sight lines open for drivers and cameras.

**Galvanized.** Zinc inside and out. No coating to chip when a forklift tine catches it.

**A deep bottom rail.** It carries the full span as a cantilever, so it is sized as a beam.

## What surrounds the gate

The gate is one item in a system.

- **Support posts and receiver post** on engineered footings.
- **Operator on a pad**, with battery backup where the site needs to exit during an outage.
- **Monitored photo eyes or edges** protecting the closing path and the run-back.
- **Covered rollers and screening** beside the travel path.
- **Vehicle loops** for free exit and to hold the gate while a truck is in the opening.
- **Access control** for staff credentials and a visitor call box.
- **Fire department access** by the method the local agency requires.
- **Bollards** protecting the operator and the pedestal.
- **A separate pedestrian gate**, so people do not walk through the vehicle opening.

The plan view shows the run-back the gate needs along the fence line.

## What would change on a real site

Usage class and cycle count set the operator. Truck turning paths set the opening and the setback. The fence the gate ties into sets height and infill. Where security risk is higher, anti-climb and anti-lift details are added.
