## The brief we set ourselves

Screen a patio from a neighboring deck without building a wall. Keep the breeze. Make something worth looking at from both sides.

## The design response

**A graded pattern.** Slots are longer and closer together in the lower two thirds, where privacy matters when people are seated. They thin out toward the top so the upper part of the screen feels light and lets sky through.

**A module.** Three identical-width panels on evenly spaced posts. A module makes the screen extendable and keeps cutting efficient.

**Standoff.** Panels mount forward of the posts on spacers. The gap throws a shadow, drains water and hides the fixings.

## Why the pattern is drawn this way

Cut patterns have rules. Enough steel has to remain between openings to keep the panel flat and strong. Long, thin strips move with heat during cutting. Staggering the slots keeps a continuous web of metal running in several directions, which is what makes a perforated panel stiff.

## Structure

A screen is a sail with holes. Posts are sized for wind on the panel's solid area and set in concrete, or base-plated to a slab or deck framing designed to take the load.

## Finish

Powder coat gives a crisp graphic read. Weathering steel gives warmth and depth and suits timber and stone. Aluminum saves weight on a deck.

## Variations

- The same pattern as gate infill or a railing panel.
- A ridgeline or tree line in place of the slots.
- Backlighting behind the standoff for evening.
- A taller version as a wind break.

## What would change on a real site

Height follows the sight line you need to block. Pattern density follows how much privacy you want. Post spacing follows the wind exposure and what the posts can be anchored to.
