## The brief we set ourselves

Design an entrance for a wooded North Idaho acreage with a ridgeline on the horizon. It should read as traditional from a distance and reward a closer look. It has to be buildable with ordinary shop processes, automated, and sound in snow.

## The design response

**One arch, two leaves.** The top rail is rolled to a single radius that runs across both leaves, so the closed gate reads as one shape. Each leaf's stile height follows the curve.

**Open above, feature below.** Vertical pickets fill the upper zone and keep the gate light and see-through at eye level. The lower zone carries the feature: a mountain profile cut from plate.

**Two layers.** A dark front plate sits forward of a warmer back plate with a gap between. The back range is taller and simpler. The front range is sharper and lower. The gap casts a real shadow that moves through the day.

**Steel does the work, stone does the framing.** Each leaf hangs from a steel post in its own footing. The pillars stand just outboard and carry lanterns and conduit, not gate load.

## Structure and hardware

The frame is welded tube with a heavier hinge stile. A mid rail divides picket and panel zones and stiffens the leaf. Barrel hinges with grease fittings sit top and bottom. A drop rod at center holds the leaves against wind. Footings are shown extending below frost depth.

In the exploded view, parts separate along the axes they would be assembled on: pickets lift out of the frame, panels come forward, posts move outward, footings drop away.

## Automation

A linear arm operator on each leaf, mounted on the property side out of sight from the road. A keypad on a gooseneck pedestal on the approach. Photo eyes across the opening. A control enclosure tucked behind a pillar.

## What would change on a real site

Everything gets checked against the ground. Opening width follows your vehicles. Arch rise follows the proportions of your pillars. The ridgeline becomes your ridgeline, redrawn from a photograph. Slope inside the gate could change it to a slide. That is what the site visit is for.
