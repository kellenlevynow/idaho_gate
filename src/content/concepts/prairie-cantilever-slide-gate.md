## The brief we set ourselves

A flat, exposed parcel. The owner wants privacy from the county road and a gate that works in February without a shovel. A solid swing gate here would be a sail on a hinge with a drift in its path.

## The design response

**Slide instead of swing.** The whole panel moves sideways along the fence line. There is no arc to keep clear.

**Cantilever instead of track.** The gate hangs from roller carriages on two posts beside the drive. Its bottom rail is both beam and track. Snow, ice and plow gravel on the driveway do not matter to it.

**Slats, not sheet.** Horizontal slats on a narrow reveal give near-full privacy from the road and still bleed off some wind. They also give the long, low lines that suit a modern house on open ground.

**A working tail.** Behind the support posts, the tail is open framing with diagonals. It counterbalances the gate and is honest about what it is.

## Structure and hardware

The bottom rail is sized to span the opening without the nose drooping. Enclosed roller trucks with sealed bearings sit on the support posts. Guide rollers at the top keep the panel upright. A receiver on the far post catches the leading edge so the closed gate is held top and bottom against wind.

The plan view shows the run-back: the opening plus the tail, which has to be straight, level and clear.

## Automation

A rack and pinion operator on a concrete pad between the support posts, with battery backup. Photo eyes across the opening. Rollers are covered and the area beside the travel path would be screened, as the gate construction standard calls for.

## Finish

Weathering steel, left to form its patina. On a real project we would plan a gravel strip under the gate line to take the rust-colored runoff of the first seasons.

## What would change on a real site

Run-back room decides which way it slides. Wind exposure sets the slat reveal. If there is not enough level ground beside the opening, a bi-parting pair halves the run on each side.
