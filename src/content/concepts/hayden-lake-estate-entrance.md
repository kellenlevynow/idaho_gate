## The brief we set ourselves

A formal entrance for a wooded lake property used in summer and at the holidays. It should be generous, quiet and classic. A caretaker and guests need access when the owners are away.

## The design response

**Proportion first.** An eighteen-foot opening needs height to match, or the gate looks squat. The arch rises well above the stiles, and the pillars are taller again, so the composition steps up toward the center and out to the caps.

**Simple infill.** Full-height pickets, evenly spaced, with one low rail. On a gate this size, restraint reads as confidence.

**Pillars as anchors.** Tall stone pillars with cast caps and lanterns. They are drawn with the gate so hinge posts, operator arms and swing all clear the stone.

## Structure

Long leaves put large loads on the hinge posts. The posts are heavy-wall steel in deep footings, independent of the masonry. The leaves are braced within the picket field so they stay flat across nine feet each. A center drop rod and stop hold the leaves in wind.

The plan view shows the swing arcs. On a wooded lot, that arc has to be kept clear of trees, boulders and snow storage.

## Automation for a seasonal home

- **Operators with battery backup**, kept charged while the house sits empty.
- **A keypad with separate codes** for the caretaker, the plow driver and guests.
- **Remote status and opening** where there is signal at the gate.
- **A hold-open setting** for arrival weekends and deliveries.
- **Emergency access** as the local fire district requires.

## What would change on a real site

The arch rise would be tuned against the real pillars. Stone would be chosen to match the house. On a sloped approach, the leaves might be uneven or the design might become a slide behind the pillars.
