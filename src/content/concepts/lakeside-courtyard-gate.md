## The brief we set ourselves

A front walk gate for a lake home. It should match an arched driveway gate nearby, close on its own behind a guest carrying bags, and still work when the walk has heaved a little after a hard winter.

## The design response

**Borrow the arch.** The top rail takes the same curve as the driveway gate, scaled to a four-foot opening. Seen together, the two read as a pair.

**Full frame, own posts.** The gate is a welded frame on two steel posts in concrete, not a panel hung off a fence post. Small gates sag for the same reasons big ones do.

**Pickets with a mid rail.** The mid rail stiffens the leaf and gives a place for a kick plate or a small cut panel.

**Hardware chosen by hand.** A lever latch that works with an elbow. Hinges that close the gate fully without slamming, and can be adjusted as seasons change.

## Details

- Bottom clearance set for the walk surface and snow.
- A positive stop so the latch is not the stop.
- Hinge side chosen so the gate swings toward the high side of any slope.
- A keyed or coded lock where the gate needs to secure the yard.

## If it guards a pool

The same gate would change in specific ways: opening away from the pool, self-latching, with the latch out of a small child's reach and picket spacing held to the barrier rules your jurisdiction applies. We confirm those before building.

## What would change on a real site

Width, if a mower or wheelchair needs to pass. Height, to match the fence or wall it sits in. Infill, to pick up a detail from the house.
