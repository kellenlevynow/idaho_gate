## The brief we set ourselves

An entrance for a working property. Trucks, stock trailers and the occasional hay load. No power at the road. It should be plain, strong and good-looking in the way useful things are.

## The design response

**Rails and a brace.** Each leaf has a tube frame, three horizontal rails and one diagonal. The diagonal runs from the bottom hinge corner to the top latch corner. In that direction it works in compression and holds the latch side up. It is the oldest trick in gate building and it is still right.

**Low and wide.** The gate is lower than an estate gate. It marks the line and stops vehicles without closing off the view.

**Heavy sections.** Frame and rails are a size up from what a decorative gate would use, because this gate will get bumped.

**Pillars for presence.** Stone pillars with lanterns turn a farm gate into an entrance. The steel posts beside them carry the load.

## Structure and hardware

Greaseable barrel hinges, sized generously. A center drop rod into a sleeve in the apron. A latch that works with a gloved hand. Footings below frost.

## Ready for solar

Shown as a manual gate. Because the leaves are open, braced and balanced, it is a good candidate for solar operators later. Planning for that now means stubbing in conduit, leaving mounting plates on the posts and frame, and choosing a spot for the panel that sees winter sun.

## Adding an arch

The natural next step is an overhead arch with the ranch name cut from plate, set tall enough for the tallest load. See the concept drawing on our [address plates and lighting](/gates/address-plates-and-lighting/) page.

## What would change on a real site

Width follows the widest implement and the turn off the road. If stock is behind the gate, the lower section gets tighter infill. On a very windy site the leaves might be a single wide gate or a slide.
