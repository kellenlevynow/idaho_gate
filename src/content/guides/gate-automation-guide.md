## Decision 1: The operator

The operator has to match the gate, so start with the gate.

**For swing gates:**

- **Linear arm.** A screw or hydraulic arm between post and leaf. The most common choice.
- **Articulated arm.** Two-piece folding arm for wide pillars or awkward geometry.
- **In-ground.** Hidden under the hinge. Needs excellent drainage.

**For sliding gates:**

- **Rack and pinion.** A motor turns a gear against a rack on the gate. Pad mounted.

**What sets the size:**

- Leaf length and weight, checked against the manufacturer's limits.
- Wind area. A solid gate loads an operator far more than pickets.
- Cycles per day and the UL 325 usage class of the site.
- Climate. Cold affects lubricants and batteries.

Buy margin. An operator at the top of its rating on a calm day is over it in a January wind.

## Decision 2: Power

| Option | Suits | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Line voltage at the gate | Heavy or busy gates | Needs a circuit run by an electrician |
| Low voltage from the house | Most homes | Transformer inside, low-voltage cable to a battery at the gate |
| Solar | Remote gates, light use | Size panel and battery for December |

Whatever the source, a **battery backup** keeps the gate working through outages, and a **manual release** lets you open it by hand. **Surge protection** is cheap insurance on rural power.

## Decision 3: Who gets in

List the people first, then choose devices.

- **Remotes and vehicle tags** for the household.
- **A keypad** with separate codes for family, regular service and guests.
- **An intercom** for visitors, ringing a station in the house or your phone.
- **A phone app** for status, remote opening and shared access.
- **Timers** for scheduled open hours.
- **Emergency access** as required by your fire district.

Check signal at the gate before buying anything that needs Wi-Fi or cellular.

## Decision 4: How people leave

Leaving should take no thought.

- **Free-exit loop or sensor.** Opens the gate as a vehicle approaches from inside.
- **Exit button** for people on foot, if the pedestrian gate is also controlled.
- **Auto-close timer** so the gate shuts behind them.

## Decision 5: Safety

This one is not optional. An automated gate is powerful enough to cause serious injury.

- **Two independent means of entrapment protection** for each place a person could be caught. Usually the operator's built-in sensing plus a monitored photo eye or edge.
- **Monitoring.** The operator checks its external devices and will not run normally if one fails.
- **Gate construction.** Covered rollers, screened openings beside a slide gate, limited gaps, no protrusions.
- **A separate pedestrian path.** People should not walk through the vehicle gate.
- **Warning signs** on both sides.

These principles come from UL 325 and ASTM F2200. Read more under [safety and compliance](/automation/safety-and-compliance/).

## Layout matters

The plan on this page shows a concept swing gate with operator arms, a photo eye beam and the swing area that must stay clear. Three layout points save a lot of grief.

- **Keypad setback.** Far enough from the gate that the longest vehicle clears the swing.
- **Driver's side.** Reachable through the window without opening the door.
- **Conduit.** Separate runs for power and low voltage, placed before concrete and paving.

## What to ask an installer

1. Which operator, and what is its rating compared with my gate's weight and length?
2. What entrapment protection devices are included, where do they go, and are they monitored?
3. What happens in a power outage?
4. How is the system protected from surges?
5. How does the fire department get in?
6. What maintenance does it need, and who does it?

If the answers are vague, keep looking.
