## Stage 1: Site visit

We meet you at the entrance. We measure the opening, check the grade with a level, look at swing or slide room, find power, and ask how you use the drive. We look at the house and anything the gate should match. We talk through type, style, automation and budget.

**You:** show us around, tell us what matters, and point out anything underground.

## Stage 2: Drawing and quote

You receive a concept drawing and a written, itemized quote. The drawing shows the gate in elevation with its dimensions, and where relevant a plan with clearances. The quote lists the gate, posts and footings, finish, automation, site work and anything optional.

We revise until it is right. Nothing is ordered or cut until you approve the drawing and the scope.

**You:** read both carefully, ask questions, and sign off. If your HOA or a fire agency needs to review the design, this is when it goes to them.

## Stage 3: Permits and locates

Where permits are needed, they are applied for now. Before any digging, public utilities are located. Private lines are yours to tell us about.

## Stage 4: Fabrication

This is the quiet stretch. In the shop, material is cut, the frame is fitted and squared on a fixture, joints are welded, infill goes in, hardware mounts are added, and welds are cleaned. The gate is test-fitted before finishing. Our [fabrication process](/company/fabrication-process/) page has the details.

**You:** nothing. If you want a progress photo, ask.

## Stage 5: Finishing

The gate is blasted or otherwise prepared, primed and coated, or sent for galvanizing first if that is in the scope. Finishing has its own lead time and cannot be rushed without paying for it later.

## Stage 6: Footings and posts

The first site day. Holes are dug to depth, below frost. Posts are set plumb and braced, and concrete is placed. Conduit for power and low voltage goes in at the same time.

Then the concrete cures. Hanging a heavy gate on green concrete is how posts end up out of plumb.

**You:** keep vehicles and curious dogs away from the braced posts.

## Stage 7: Hanging the gate

The gate arrives finished. It is lifted into place, hung on its hinges or set on its rollers, and adjusted until it is level, plumb and closes cleanly. Stops, latches and drop rods are fitted. At this point you have a working manual gate.

## Stage 8: Automation

Operators are mounted and wired. Safety devices, the keypad, intercom, exit sensor and any locks are installed. Power is connected, by a licensed electrician where a new circuit is involved. Then the system is set up: travel limits, force, timing and codes. Every safety device is tested.

## Stage 9: Finishing touches

Pillars, caps, lighting and the address plate, if they are part of the project. Masonry may run before or after the gate is hung depending on the design.

## Stage 10: Walkthrough and handover

We show you:

- How to open and close the gate every way it can be opened.
- How to add, change and remove codes and users.
- Where the manual release is and how to use it.
- How to test the safety devices.
- What to check each season.

You get the operator manuals and our contact details for service.

**You:** try everything yourself while we are there. Ask the question you think is too basic.

## After that

Keep to the [maintenance checklist](/resources/gate-maintenance-checklist/). Call if anything changes in how the gate moves or sounds.
