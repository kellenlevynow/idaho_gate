## What you need

A tape measure of 25 feet or longer, a phone for photos and its level app, and something to write on. A second person makes the long measurements easier.

## The six measurements

The diagram on this page labels each one.

### A. Clear opening

Measure between the two points where gate posts or pillars will stand, at ground level. If there are existing posts, measure between their inside faces. If there are none, measure between the things that fix the width: fence ends, walls, trees, the edges of a culvert.

Measure again three feet up. If the two numbers differ, note both.

### B. Driveway width

The paved or graveled width at the gate line. The opening can be wider than the driveway and usually should be.

### C. Slope

Stand at the gate line and look inward. Does the driveway rise, fall or stay level over the first 15 to 20 feet? Then look across the opening from one side to the other. Is one side higher?

Estimate the rise in inches over a known distance. A phone level app on a straight board gives you degrees, which works too.

### D. Swing room

From the gate line, measure straight inward along each edge of the drive. You need at least the length of a leaf, which is half the opening for a double gate and the full opening for a single. Note anything in that area: a slope, a parked vehicle spot, a tree, a wall.

### E. Slide room

From each side of the opening, measure along the fence line. A cantilever sliding gate needs roughly one and a half times the opening width on one side, level and clear. Note which side has the room.

### F. Setback from the road

Measure from the edge of the road to the gate line. Then think about the longest vehicle that will stop there while the gate opens. If it would hang into the road, the gate needs to move back.

## Three more things to note

- **Power.** Where is the nearest outlet or panel, and about how far is it from the gate? What is in between: lawn, pavement, rock?
- **What is underground.** Septic, irrigation, propane or power lines near the gate.
- **Snow.** Where the plow pushes it, and where it drifts.

## Photos that help

1. From the road, looking straight at the opening.
2. From inside, looking back toward the road.
3. Along the fence line to the left and to the right.
4. The ground at each post location.
5. The house or any fence or stone you want the gate to match.

Include something for scale in the wide shots. A person or a vehicle works.

## What your numbers are telling you

| What you found | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Level inside, plenty of swing room | A swing gate will work |
| Drive climbs inside the gate | Slide, or a raked swing gate |
| No swing room, long clear fence line | Cantilever slide |
| No swing room, no slide room | Bi-fold, or relocate the gate |
| Opening over 20 ft | Double swing or slide |
| Short setback from a busy road | Move the gate back, or plan for fast opening |
| Power far away | Low-voltage run, or solar |
