## Read this first

Rules for gates are set locally and they change. This guide describes the questions that come up across North Idaho, Eastern Washington and Western Montana. It is not legal advice and it does not state the rule for your address. We confirm requirements with the relevant offices during quoting, and you should too.

## 1. The building department

Your city, or the county if you are outside city limits.

**Ask:**

- Does a driveway gate need a permit here?
- Is there a height limit for gates, fences and pillars in a front yard?
- Do masonry pillars or walls over a certain height need a permit or engineering?
- What is required for the electrical work to power an operator?
- Are there setback rules from the property line?

Electrical work is commonly permitted separately and done by a licensed electrician. In Washington, electrical permitting is handled at the state level in many areas.

## 2. The road agency

Whoever owns the road your driveway meets. That might be the city, a county road department, a highway district, or the state transportation department for a state highway.

**Ask:**

- Where is the edge of the right of way? It is often well inside where the pavement or gravel ends.
- Does anything need to stay out of the right of way? Usually yes: gates, pillars, keypads.
- Is there a sight distance or clear zone requirement at the driveway?
- Does changing the driveway approach need a permit?

A gate or pillar placed in the right of way can be ordered removed. Find the line first.

## 3. The fire agency

The city fire department or the fire district that covers your address.

Model fire codes, which most jurisdictions adopt in some form, say that a gate across a fire apparatus access road must be approved by the fire code official. In practice, agencies commonly look at:

- **Clear width** when the gate is open. A figure of 20 feet is common in model code appendices for fire access roads. Your agency may differ.
- **Emergency opening.** A key switch or lock box of the type the agency uses.
- **Operation without power.** A way to open the gate in an outage.
- **Setback and turnaround** so apparatus is not stopped in the road.
- **Maintenance.** The gate must be kept working.

Whether your driveway counts as a fire apparatus access road depends on its length, what it serves and local rules. Long drives, shared drives and private roads are the usual cases. The plan on this page shows a concept gate with its clearances drawn.

**Ask:**

- Does my driveway fall under your access requirements?
- What minimum clear width do you require at a gate?
- What emergency access device do you require, and how do I order it?
- Do you need to review or inspect the gate?

## 4. Your HOA or road association

If you are in a subdivision or on a shared private road.

**Ask:**

- Are driveway gates allowed?
- What are the limits on height, material, color and location?
- What does the architectural committee need to see, and how long does review take?
- On a shared road: does everyone who uses the road need to agree, and how do they get access?

We prepare scaled drawings, a site plan and finish samples for committee review.

## 5. Before any digging

Call for utility locates. In Idaho, Washington and Montana that is a free service reached by dialing 811. Private lines such as irrigation, septic, propane and power to outbuildings are not marked by the public locate, so tell us where yours run.

## 6. Across state lines

Contractor registration, electrical rules and sales tax differ between Idaho, Washington and Montana. If your project is in Washington or Montana, ask any contractor for their registration in that state and verify it with the state agency.

## A simple order of operations

1. Find your jurisdiction and your right-of-way line.
2. Call the fire agency if the drive is long, shared or serves more than one home.
3. Read your covenants.
4. Get a drawing.
5. Submit what needs submitting.
6. Call 811.
7. Build.
