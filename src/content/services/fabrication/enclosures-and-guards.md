## Built for the way it gets used

Site steel fails in predictable ways. Enclosure gates sag because the leaf is ten feet wide and the hinges came from a farm store. Bollards lean because they were set in a post hole with a bag of mix. Guards get removed because they make the job harder. Each of those is a design problem, and each has a known fix.

## Enclosures

A good enclosure does three jobs: it screens, it secures and it stays out of the way of whoever services what is inside.

- **Gate leaves are braced** so they stay flat at full width.
- **Hinge posts are heavy** and set in real footings.
- **Hinges are industrial**, greaseable and sized for the leaf.
- **Hold-opens are built in.** Cane bolts with sleeves in the slab, or latching stays.
- **Infill suits the purpose.** Slats for screening with airflow, mesh for security, plate for full concealment.
- **Protection is included.** Bollards at the corners and beside the posts.

The drawing on this page shows a concept enclosure with a double slatted gate, cane bolts and a bollard.

## Bollards and vehicle protection

Bollards protect things that are expensive or dangerous to hit: gas meters, electrical gear, storefronts, gate operators, pedestals, building corners, pedestrians.

- **Embedded bollards** are set in concrete below grade.
- **Surface-mounted bollards** bolt to a slab where digging is not possible.
- **Removable bollards** drop into sleeves for occasional access.
- **Guard rail** protects a length of wall, racking or equipment.

## Guards, cages and barriers

For shops, plants and yards we build framed guards around moving equipment, cages for storage and tool cribs, and barriers to separate people from vehicles. Guards that are easy to remove for maintenance and easy to put back are the ones that stay on.

Hot-dip galvanizing is the workhorse finish for site steel. Powder coat over galvanizing adds color for architectural settings. Safety yellow where it needs to be seen. See [materials and finishes](/company/materials-finishes/).
