## The jobs without a name

Every fabrication shop has a list of things it built once. A steel base for a twelve-foot slab table. A rack for drying waders at a fishing lodge. A fixture to hold a boat motor. A replacement bracket for a 1960s tractor. A stair rail for a spiral that was laid out by eye in 1978.

None of those fit a product page. All of them use the same skills: understand what it has to do, draw it, build it, make it look like it was meant to be there.

## What we like to see

- **A clear purpose.** What does it hold, carry, cover or support?
- **The setting.** Indoors or out, public or private, seen or hidden.
- **The constraints.** Size, weight, budget, date.
- **Something to look at.** A sketch, a photo of something similar, the space it goes in.

That is enough for a first conversation. The stair railing drawing on this page is a concept, but it is typical of the work: geometry that has to be measured on site and metal that has to follow it.

## How one-offs get built

1. **Talk it through.** Often on site or in the shop.
2. **Sketch and budget.** A rough design and a rough number, so you can decide whether to go on.
3. **Drawing and quote.** A scaled drawing and a firm price.
4. **First article.** For multiples or tricky pieces, we build one and review it with you.
5. **Build and finish.**

## Where we draw the line

We are a steel and aluminum fabrication shop. We do not do production machining, casting or sheet metal ductwork, and we are not the right shop for pressure vessels or work that needs certifications we do not hold. If your project needs one of those, we will tell you, and where we can we will point you to someone who does it.
