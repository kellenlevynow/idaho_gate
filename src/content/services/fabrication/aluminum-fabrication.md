## Where aluminum earns its place

North Idaho has a lot of water. Coeur d'Alene, Hayden, Pend Oreille, Priest and dozens of smaller lakes are lined with docks, and every one of them has a gangway, a rail and a ladder that sit in splash and sun all year. Steel in that setting is a maintenance schedule. Aluminum is not.

The same logic applies away from the water.

- **Wide gate leaves.** Less weight means smaller hinges, lighter posts and a smaller operator.
- **Solar gates.** Every pound moved costs stored energy.
- **Anything carried or lifted.** Ramps, racks, removable panels, hatch covers.
- **Railings over finished surfaces.** No rust streaks on stone or composite decking.

## Designing in aluminum

Aluminum is not light steel. Three differences drive the design.

- **Stiffness.** It deflects roughly three times as much as steel under the same load in the same shape. Sections get deeper or walls get thicker.
- **Welds.** Heat softens the metal near a weld in common structural alloys. We place welds where stress is lower and size them to suit.
- **Dissimilar metals.** Aluminum against steel or copper, plus water, corrodes. We separate them.

## Finish options

Left bare, aluminum weathers to a soft gray. Brushed, it has a clean grain. Powder coat gives color and extra protection. We go through the choices in [materials and finishes](/company/materials-finishes/).

## Waterfront notes

Docks and shoreline structures are often regulated by the state, the county or a lake association. If your project touches the water, check what approvals apply before ordering fabrication. We build to the dimensions and details your permit allows.
