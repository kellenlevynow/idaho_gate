## Steel that has to fit the first time

Structural and miscellaneous steel arrives on a site where a crane, a crew and a concrete truck are waiting. If a base plate hole pattern is off by a quarter inch, everybody stops. Our job is to make sure it is not.

That starts with reading the drawings properly. We check the structural sheets against the architectural ones, flag conflicts before cutting, and confirm field dimensions on anything that ties into existing work.

## What we fabricate

- **Columns and posts** with base and cap plates.
- **Beams and headers**, including flitch plates and built-up sections.
- **Moment and portal frames** for wide openings.
- **Lintels and ledger angles.**
- **Embeds, anchor assemblies and hold-downs.**
- **Stair stringers, landings and support steel.**
- **Canopy and awning frames.**
- **Equipment platforms and supports.**

The drawing on this page is a concept frame: beam, columns, knee braces and base plates on anchor rods. On a real job every member size and connection comes from the engineer of record.

## Shop practice

- **Material is traceable** to the order, and to mill certs when the spec calls for them.
- **Parts are cut and drilled from the drawing**, on CNC equipment where the shape allows.
- **Fit-up is checked** before welding.
- **Welds follow the specified size and process.**
- **Pieces are marked** to the drawings so the site crew knows where each one goes.

## Exposed steel

When structure is also the finish, the standard changes. Welds are ground or laid for appearance. Mill marks are removed. Joints line up. We agree on a finish standard with the architect or owner before starting, because "exposed" means different things to different people.

## Finish and corrosion

Interior steel is usually shop primed. Exterior and buried steel should be galvanized or coated for the exposure. We go through the choices in [materials and finishes](/company/materials-finishes/).
