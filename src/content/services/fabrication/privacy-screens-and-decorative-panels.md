## Pattern with a purpose

The ridgeline panel in the gate on our [home page](/) is a decorative panel. So is an address plate, a ranch sign, and a screen that hides a propane tank. They are all the same process: draw, cut, clean, finish, mount.

What separates a good one from a clip-art one is the drawing. We draw panels for the place they are going. A ridgeline can be the one you see from your deck. A pattern can pick up a motif from the house. A sign can use lettering that suits the business instead of whatever font came with the software.

## What panels are used for

- **Privacy** around patios, hot tubs, outdoor showers and property lines.
- **Screening** of utilities, bins, tanks and mechanical equipment.
- **Shade and wind control** on decks and in courtyards.
- **Infill** in gates, fences and railings.
- **Facade and wall features** on homes and commercial buildings.
- **Signs and identity.**

The drawing on this page shows a concept three-panel screen between tube posts, with a pattern that thins toward the top.

## Design rules that keep panels flat and strong

- **Leave enough steel.** Open area looks light, but the remaining web carries the panel.
- **Avoid long, thin fingers.** They move with heat and catch on things.
- **Manage heat.** Cutting puts heat into the sheet. Sequence and layout keep it from warping.
- **Frame or stiffen large panels.** An unframed sheet above a certain size will oil-can.
- **Stand panels off.** A gap behind the panel adds shadow and lets water and debris fall through.

## Layers

Two or three plates stacked with spacers turn a flat picture into a relief. A dark front layer over a weathering steel or bronze back layer is a combination we like. You can see it in the exploded view of the concept gate, where the front ridgeline plate separates from the standoff layer behind it.

## Material and finish

- **Powder-coated steel** for crisp color and long life.
- **Weathering steel** for a patina that suits rural and modern settings. Plan for runoff during the first seasons.
- **Aluminum** for weight savings and waterfront use.
- **Backlighting** behind a standoff panel for entrances and signs.

## From idea to panel

Send a photo, a sketch or a description and the size of the space. We return a scaled proof. Once you approve it, the panel is cut, edges are cleaned, and it is finished and mounted.
