## Code first, then design

Railings are life-safety items. Building codes set their height, the size of the gaps, the load they must resist and the shape of a graspable handrail. Those rules are the starting grid for every design, and they are enforced at inspection.

The figures in the FAQ on this page come from the model codes most jurisdictions adopt. Local amendments exist. We check before we draw.

## Choosing an infill

- **Cable** keeps the view. It needs stiff, well-anchored end posts, because all the tension lands there. The drawing on this page shows a concept cable guard with a flat bar top rail.
- **Pickets** are the most economical and the hardest to climb.
- **Horizontal bar or rod** suits modern homes.
- **Mesh or perforated panel** gives a commercial, industrial look and good fall protection.
- **Cut panels** turn the railing into a feature. See [decorative panels](/fabrication/privacy-screens-and-decorative-panels/).
- **Glass-ready frames** are built to receive glazing by a glass contractor.

## The connection is the railing

A guard is only as strong as what it is attached to. A post plate lagged into the edge of a deck board will fail the load test and, eventually, a person. We look at the structure first.

- **Wood decks.** Posts tie into framing or blocking, not just decking.
- **Concrete.** Core drilled and grouted, or plates on proper anchors.
- **Steel stairs.** Welded or bolted to stringers.
- **Fascia mounts.** Keep the walking surface clear and move the load into the rim.

## Stair rails

Stairs add geometry. The rail has to follow the pitch, hold a consistent height above the nosings, return at the ends, and on commercial stairs extend past the top and bottom steps. We template or measure the actual stair, because the as-built pitch is rarely what the drawing says.

## Snow country details

- Top rails that shed water instead of holding it.
- Open ends capped or drained so tube does not fill and freeze.
- Posts and anchors that tolerate a roof avalanche landing nearby.
- Finishes that stand up to de-icing salt on steps.

## Residential and commercial

For homes we handle design, measure, fabrication and install. For builders and commercial projects we work from the drawings, provide shop drawings and coordinate with the framing and finish schedule. Commercial rails bring accessibility requirements for handrail size, clearance and extensions, which we build to as specified.
