## Where structure meets finish

Hidden steel only has to be strong. Exposed steel has to be strong, straight, consistent and clean. Joints line up. Welds are either dressed flush or laid as a deliberate bead. Fasteners are chosen and placed on purpose. That takes more time in the shop and more care on site, and it is the difference between metalwork and steel.

## Work we take on

- **Entry canopies and awnings.** The drawing on this page shows a concept canopy hung from the wall on tie rods.
- **Entry arches and overhead signs** for ranches, estates, parks and businesses.
- **Trellises, pergolas and shade structures.**
- **Steel stairs.** Stringers, pan treads, floating treads, landings.

- **Feature walls and screens.** See [decorative panels](/fabrication/privacy-screens-and-decorative-panels/).

## How we work with design teams

1. **Review.** We read the drawings and list questions and options.
2. **Shop drawings.** Every piece, joint, anchor and finish, for approval.
3. **Samples.** Finish samples and, on demanding work, a mockup of a typical joint.
4. **Fabrication.** Built in the shop in the largest pieces that can be moved and installed.
5. **Install.** Coordinated with the builder so anchors, blocking and adjacent finishes are ready.

## Snow, water and movement

Exterior metalwork here has to deal with three things.

- **Snow load.** A canopy or trellis collects snow. Loads are set by your jurisdiction and are not small.
- **Water.** Steel that traps water rusts from the inside. We drain tube, cap ends and slope flat surfaces.
- **Thermal movement.** Long pieces grow and shrink through the year. Connections have to allow it.

## Finishes

Exterior work is usually galvanized, powder coated, or both. Weathering steel is an option where runoff staining can be managed. Interior work opens up clear-coated raw steel and patinas. See [materials and finishes](/company/materials-finishes/).

## Tying it to the entrance

Many architectural projects start at the gate and work inward: the same cut pattern on the gate, the address plate and a courtyard screen, or the same top profile on the gate and the porch rail. Designing those together is where a custom shop does its best work.
