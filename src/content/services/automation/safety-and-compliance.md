## Why this matters

Gate operators are strong enough to move a thousand pounds of steel. Injuries happen when a person is caught between a moving gate and something fixed: a post, a wall, the ground, the other leaf. Standards call those places entrapment zones. The whole safety system is built around detecting a person in one of those zones and stopping the gate.

This page explains the principles. It is not a substitute for the standards themselves or the operator manufacturer's instructions, which govern every installation.

## Two standards, two jobs

**UL 325** covers the operator. It sets how operators detect obstructions, what external devices they must work with, and how they behave when a device fails.

**ASTM F2200** covers the gate. It sets how the gate itself must be built so it does not create hazards.

They are meant to be used together. Industry guidance from the Door and Access Systems Manufacturers Association (DASMA) is a good plain-language reference for both.

## What the operator standard asks for

- **Two independent means of entrapment protection** for each entrapment zone. Typically the operator's inherent sensing plus a photo eye or an edge.
- **Monitoring.** External devices are checked by the operator. If one is missing or faulty, the operator will not run normally.
- **Protection in both directions** wherever an entrapment zone exists on opening and on closing.
- **Controls in sight of the gate**, so nobody resets or runs it blind.

## What the gate standard asks for

- **Covered rollers** on sliding gates.
- **Screening** of openings beside a sliding gate's path, so hands cannot reach through.
- **Limited gaps** between the gate and fixed objects.
- **No protrusions** that could catch a person, and smooth bottom edges on swing gates.
- **A gate that stays put** if it is disconnected from the operator.
- **A separate pedestrian entrance.** People on foot should not use the vehicle gate.

## Where the devices go

Every site is different, but the logic is the same: find each place a person could be caught, then protect it. On a swing gate that includes the closing path and the space behind an opening leaf near a wall or pillar. On a slide gate it includes the closing path, the receiver post, and the run-back area where the tail passes the support posts. ## Vehicle detection is a separate job

Loops and vehicle sensors keep the gate from closing on a car. They do not reliably detect a person, so they are added to entrapment protection, not counted as part of it.

## Testing and upkeep

Safety devices only work if they work. Photo eyes get knocked out of line, lenses get dirty, edges get damaged. Test them regularly using the method in the operator manual, and have the system checked if anything behaves oddly.
