## Start with who needs to get in

Access control goes wrong when it is chosen from a catalog instead of from a list of people. Before picking hardware, we write down who uses the gate.

- **Household.** Every day, in vehicles, often with full hands.
- **Regular service.** Cleaners, landscapers, plow drivers, propane delivery.
- **Guests.** Occasional, unfamiliar with the property.
- **Parcels and freight.** Frequent, time-limited, in a hurry.
- **Emergency crews.** Rare, and cannot wait.

Each group gets the simplest method that works for it.

## The devices

**Keypads** are the workhorse. They need no phone, no battery in your pocket and no network. Good ones are backlit, weather sealed and mounted where a driver can reach them without getting out. Multiple codes let you give the plow driver access without giving up your own code.

**Remotes and vehicle tags** are fastest for daily use. A visor remote opens the gate before you stop.

**Intercoms** handle visitors. Audio is enough for many homes. Video helps with deliveries and adds a record. Cellular units call your phone and need no wiring back to the house.

**App control** adds status, sharing and remote opening. It depends on connectivity at the gate.

**Exit sensors** let anyone inside leave freely, which is what most households want.

## Placement is half of it

A keypad on the wrong side of the drive, or so close to the gate that a truck has to back up to let the leaf swing, will annoy you forever. We place the pedestal on the driver's side, set back far enough for the longest vehicle and the swing, at a height that works for a car and a pickup, and lit so the keys can be seen at night.

## Scheduling and holding open

Most controllers can hold the gate open on a schedule: weekday mornings, a party, a construction project. A simple hold-open switch at the house is worth having for snow days.

## Commercial and community sites

Shared entrances need credentials that can be issued and revoked, a visitor call path, and often a log. Operators accept signals from most third-party access systems, so we can work with your security integrator or supply a telephone-entry system directly.
