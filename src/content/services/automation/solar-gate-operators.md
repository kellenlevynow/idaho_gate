## When solar is the right answer

The gate sits a long way from the house. The utility would need a new service, or the trench crosses rock, a creek or a paved road. Use is modest: a household and a few visitors. In those cases solar is not a compromise. It is the sensible design.

When the gate is heavy, busy or loaded with accessories, utility power is usually better. We will tell you which side of the line your gate is on.

## The energy budget

A solar gate lives on a budget. Energy comes in through the panel on bright hours. It goes out two ways.

- **Movement.** Each open and close uses a burst of power. A light, balanced gate uses little.
- **Standby.** The control board and every accessory draw a small amount around the clock.

In summer the panel wins easily. In December at this latitude the sun is low, days are short and storms can run for a week. The battery has to carry the gate through that stretch, and the panel has to refill it when the sky clears.

So we size for winter. That means more panel and more battery than a summer calculation suggests, and discipline about standby loads.

## Getting the panel right

- **Tilt it steeply.** It catches low winter sun and sheds snow.
- **Keep it out of shade.** A tree shadow at noon in December can cut output to almost nothing.
- **Mount it where the sun is.** The panel does not have to be on the gate post.
- **Keep it reachable.** Someone will want to brush it off after a heavy, wet snow.

## Make the gate easy to move

Every pound and every bit of friction costs energy. Solar gates should be as light as the design allows, hung on good hinges, and balanced so they stay put by hand. Open infill beats solid panels, because wind load is an energy cost too. Aluminum is worth considering for wide leaves. See [aluminum fabrication](/fabrication/aluminum-fabrication/).

## Access control on solar

Keypads and remotes draw very little. Cellular intercoms vary, so we check standby figures. Magnetic locks draw continuously and are a poor fit; a mechanical or motorized lock is better. The [automation guide](/resources/gate-automation-guide/) has more on choosing devices.
