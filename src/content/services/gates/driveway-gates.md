## Built for the opening you actually have

No two driveways give you the same thing to work with. One is a flat gravel apron on the Rathdrum Prairie with nothing to stop the wind. The next is a paved switchback above Hayden Lake with eight feet between the road and the first curve. A catalog gate assumes a level pad and a standard width. Yours probably has neither.

We measure the opening, shoot the grade, and look at where the gate has to go when it is open. That decides the type before anyone talks about style.

- **Width and height** set the weight of the leaf and the size of the posts.
- **Grade** decides swing or slide, and whether the bottom rail needs to rake.
- **Setback from the road** decides whether a vehicle can pull fully off the road before the gate opens.
- **Snow storage** decides where a plow can push without burying the gate or the operator.

## What goes into the frame

A gate is a cantilever. Every pound in the leaf hangs off one edge and tries to pull the latch side toward the ground. We build frames from steel tube with fully welded joints, not tacked corners. Hinge stiles are heavier than latch stiles. Wide leaves get a diagonal or a truss member so they stay flat for decades, not seasons.

Posts matter as much as the gate. A hinge post is a lever arm set in the ground. We size the post and its footing to the leaf, set the footing below local frost depth, and let the concrete cure before the gate hangs on it.

## Infill and style

The frame carries the load. The infill carries the look. Vertical pickets are the classic choice and keep sight lines open. Horizontal slats suit modern homes. Solid plate or timber gives privacy but catches wind, so the frame, hinges and operator all step up to match. Cut panels let you put a ridgeline, a brand or a pattern into the gate itself.

You can try combinations in the [gate studio](/studio/) before we ever meet. It is a starting point, not a final drawing.

## From first call to a working gate

1. **Site visit.** We measure, check grade and soil, and look at power and access.
2. **Drawing and quote.** You get a dimensioned drawing and a written, itemized price.
3. **Fabrication.** Cut, fit, weld and finish in the shop.
4. **Install.** Footings, posts, hang, align. Then automation and a walkthrough.

## Plan for automation now, even if you add it later

The cheapest time to run conduit is before the concrete goes in. If there is any chance you will add an operator, keypad or intercom, say so up front. We will leave the gate ready for it.
