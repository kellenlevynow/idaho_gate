## Small gate, same standards

A walk gate is light enough that people assume it is easy. Then it drags on the walk in February, or the latch stops catching in August when the steel has grown in the sun. Small gates fail for the same reasons big ones do: a post that moved, a frame that racked, hardware that was not up to the job.

We build pedestrian gates as full frames with their own posts, set in concrete like their larger siblings. The gate on this page is a concept with an arched top and picket infill, drawn the same way we draw a 20 foot entrance.

## Hardware you touch every day

- **Latches.** A lever latch works with an elbow when your hands are full. A thumb latch is traditional. A magnetic latch closes quietly and holds in wind.
- **Self-closing hinges.** Spring or hydraulic. Adjustable, so the gate closes fully without slamming.
- **Stops.** A positive stop protects the latch and keeps the gate from swinging through.
- **Locks.** Keyed deadbolt, coded mechanical lock, or electric strike tied to an access system.

## Matching the rest of the property

A walk gate should look like it belongs to something. We match the driveway gate's top profile and infill, pick up a railing detail from the porch, or repeat a cut pattern from a [privacy screen](/fabrication/privacy-screens-and-decorative-panels/). Because the gate is custom, it also fits the opening you have, whether that is a masonry arch, a gap in a hedge or the end of a retaining wall.

## Ground and clearance

Walks heave, pavers settle and snow builds up. We set bottom clearance for your surface and your winters, and hang the gate so it swings toward the high side of any slope.

## Access for people who are not you

Dog walkers, landscapers and house guests need a way in that does not involve a hidden key. A keypad with codes you can change, or a lock tied to your phone, solves that. See [access control](/automation/access-control/).
