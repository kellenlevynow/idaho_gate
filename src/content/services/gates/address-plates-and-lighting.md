## An address people can actually find

Rural addresses are hard. Drives are long, mailboxes cluster at the county road, and phone maps guess. A clear address at the gate helps guests and delivery drivers. For fire, medical and sheriff's crews it can save minutes.

We cut address plates from steel or aluminum plate on our CNC plasma table. Because we draw them ourselves, the numbers can be any size or style, and the plate can carry more than digits: a family name, a ridgeline, a brand.

## Design choices

- **Cut-through numbers** read as dark shapes against stone by day and can be backlit at night.
- **Raised numbers** on a solid plate give the strongest daytime contrast.
- **Standoff mounting** puts a shadow gap behind the plate, which adds depth and lets water drain.
- **Finish** usually matches the gate. A contrasting finish helps legibility.

## Light it properly

Good entrance lighting does three jobs. It marks the entrance from a distance. It lets a driver see the keypad and the edges of the drive. And it lets a camera or an intercom see a face. It should do all three without throwing glare into the road or the neighbor's bedroom.

- **Pillar lanterns** mark the entrance and give warm ambient light.
- **Shielded downlights** under caps or arches light the plate and the apron.
- **Backlighting** behind a standoff plate makes the address float.
- **A keypad light** makes codes usable in the dark.

Fixtures are low-voltage LED on a photocell or timer. In the theater on our [home page](/) you can see lanterns on a concept gate.

## Signs and arches

For ranches and estates, the address often grows into a sign. An overhead arch with a cut panel is the traditional form. The drawing on this page shows a concept. We set arch height for the tallest vehicle the drive will see, and we treat the posts like gate posts: steel in concrete, below frost.

## Built in, not bolted on

Lighting and signage go best when they are decided with the gate. Conduit goes into the pillars before the stone. Mounting points are welded on before the finish. Adding them later works, but it shows.
