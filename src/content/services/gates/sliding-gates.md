## When a slide is the right gate

- The driveway rises or falls inside the gate.
- There is no room for a swing arc, or cars park where the arc would be.
- The opening is wide. Past 20 feet, a slide is often simpler than two long leaves.
- The gate is solid or semi-solid and the site is windy.
- Snow in the swing arc is going to be a fight all winter.

If two or more of those describe your entrance, start here.

## How a cantilever gate works

The gate is longer than the opening. The extra length, called the tail, stays behind two support posts set beside the driveway. Enclosed roller carriages on those posts run inside the gate's bottom rail, and guide rollers at the top keep it upright. The opening half hangs in the air, counterbalanced by the tail. Nothing touches the driveway.

The elevation drawing on this page shows the tail and the diagonal bracing that keeps it from sagging. The plan view shows how far the gate runs back when it opens.

## What we build into it

- **A stiff bottom rail.** It is both the track and the beam. We size it to the span so the nose does not droop at full extension.
- **Sealed roller trucks.** Bearings rated for the gate weight, adjustable for height and alignment.
- **A receiver post.** The leading edge lands in a catch so the closed gate is held top and bottom.
- **Bracing in the tail.** Diagonals put the tail in tension and keep the whole panel straight.
- **A clear path.** The run-back area is graded and kept free of drift and stored snow.

## Automation and safety

Slide operators drive a rack bolted to the gate. They sit on a concrete pad beside the support posts with a battery backup for outages. Because a sliding gate passes close to fixed posts and fence, safety rules for gaps, guards and sensors are specific. Rollers are covered, openings near the travel path are screened, and monitored sensors protect both directions of travel. Read [safety and compliance](/automation/safety-and-compliance/) for the details.

## Planning the fence line

The run-back needs a straight, level strip along the fence, clear of trees, utilities and grade changes. On tight lots we look at bi-parting or telescoping designs. On acreage it is rarely an issue. The [measuring guide](/resources/measuring-your-opening/) shows what to check before our visit.
