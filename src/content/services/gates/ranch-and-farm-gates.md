## Built for work first

Acreage gates see loads that suburban gates do not. A feed truck clips a post. A trailer wheel rides the edge of the apron. Snow gets pushed against the leaf by a tractor bucket. We build ranch gates from heavier wall steel than a decorative gate needs, with bracing you can see and hinges you can grease.

The classic form is horizontal rails with one diagonal brace. It is not decoration. The brace runs from the bottom hinge corner to the top latch corner and holds the leaf square by putting steel in compression. The drawing on this page shows it on a concept double gate.

## The approach matters as much as the gate

Most gate damage on rural drives happens on the turn in. We look at:

- **Setback from the road**, so a truck and trailer can pull fully off before stopping.
- **Turning room**, which often calls for a wider opening than the road width suggests.
- **Apron and drainage**, so the swing area does not become a mud hole or an ice sheet.
- **Fence tie-ins**, so the gate posts and the fence posts are not fighting each other.

## Entry arches and signs

An overhead arch turns a gate into an entrance. We set two posts in concrete, span them with a beam, and hang a sign panel cut from plate. Lettering, brands and scenery are drawn in our software and cut on the CNC plasma table, so the sign is yours and not a stock font on a stock blank. See [architectural metalwork](/fabrication/architectural-metalwork/) for arches without gates.

## Automation that suits acreage

A gate half a mile from the house changes the automation plan.

- **Solar power** avoids trenching a long electrical run.
- **Long-range remotes, keypads and app control** let you open it from the cab.
- **Timers** can hold the gate open through chore hours and close it at night.
- **Exit sensors** let vehicles leave without a code.

Read the [automation guide](/resources/gate-automation-guide/) for how those pieces fit.

## Finishes that hold up

Powder coat over galvanizing is the long-life choice for gates that take abuse. Weathering steel suits ranch settings and needs no coating. Plain painted steel is the budget option and needs upkeep. We cover the trade-offs in the [materials guide](/resources/materials-guide/).
