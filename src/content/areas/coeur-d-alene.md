## Gates for the way Coeur d'Alene is built

The city sits at the north end of the lake with hills on three sides. That geography produces three common situations.

**Hillside and lake-view lots.** Drives are paved, steep and short. Setbacks are tight and neighbors are close. Gates here need careful layout: where the gate goes when open, where a car waits, where snow is stored. Sliding gates and compact double swings are typical. Near the water, aluminum is worth a look.

**In-town lots.** Older neighborhoods near downtown and the lake have alleys, sidewalks and narrow frontages. A gate here is often lower and lighter, and a courtyard or pedestrian gate may matter as much as the vehicle gate. Sight lines at the sidewalk and the street need attention.

**Acreage in the hills.** South and west of town, and around Dalton Gardens, parcels get larger and drives get longer. Wide double swing gates between pillars suit these properties, and power may be a long way from the gate.

## Local planning notes

- **Know your jurisdiction.** City limits are irregular here. The same road can pass from city to county and back.
- **Driveway approaches.** Outside city limits, roads are maintained by highway districts, and work at the edge of the road goes through them.
- **Fire access.** If the drive serves a home beyond a certain length or more than one home, the fire agency may have width and emergency access requirements for a gate. Ask before you build.
- **HOAs.** Several hillside and golf communities have design review. We prepare the drawings a committee needs.
