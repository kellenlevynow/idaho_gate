## A growing city with three kinds of property

Census Bureau estimates put Post Falls among the fastest-growing cities in Idaho, and most of that growth is new housing. For gates, that means three typical projects.

**Subdivision homes.** Lots are regular, drives are short and paved, and there is usually an HOA. Gates are modest in height, set back from the sidewalk, and designed to pass review. Automation is nearly always wanted, and conduit is easiest to place before landscaping goes in.

**River and hillside properties.** Along the Spokane River and on the high ground south of the interstate, lots are larger and more varied. Slope and views drive the design, as they do around the lake.

**Prairie acreage.** North toward Hauser and the Rathdrum Prairie, parcels are flat, open and exposed. Long, wide gates are common. Wind and drifting snow are the design conditions.

## Getting through design review

An architectural committee usually wants to see four things.

1. A scaled elevation of the gate and any pillars.
2. A site plan showing location and setback.
3. Materials and finish, with a color sample.
4. Details of any lighting or signage.

We produce those as part of the quote process. The concept drawings on this site show the format.

## Planning notes

- **Setback.** Allow room for a vehicle to stop fully off the street and clear of the sidewalk while the gate opens.
- **Utilities.** Newer lots have utilities in the front easement. Call for locates before any digging. We do.
- **Power.** A garage circuit is often close enough to feed a low-voltage operator.
- **Fire access.** Shared drives and long private lanes may have requirements from the fire agency.
