## Where we go, and how

North Idaho's western edge is a line on a map. Newport and Oldtown share a main street. The Pend Oreille River valley, the lakes north of Spokane and the wheat country to the south all sit within reach of our shop.

We treat this as project territory. That means we do not promise the same response as in our home county, and we say so plainly.

- **Near the line.** Newport, Diamond Lake, Elk, Chattaroy, Deer Park. Reachable on a normal route.
- **South and west.** Cheney, Medical Lake, the Palouse. Evaluated job by job.
- **Farther.** Usually fabricate and deliver, with a local installer.

## Work that travels well

**Larger gates.** A wide cantilever slide gate or a full estate entrance justifies the mobilization. The drawing on this page shows a concept commercial slide gate.

**Commercial and agricultural projects.** Yards, plants, farms and facilities with defined scope and schedule.

**Shop fabrication.** Railings, panels, brackets, signs and frames built to dimensions and delivered.

## Fabricate here, install there

For many distant jobs the best arrangement is a split.

1. You or your contractor measure, or we visit once to measure.
2. We draw it and you approve the drawing.
3. We fabricate and finish in the shop.
4. It is delivered or picked up.
5. A local crew installs it.

Our guide to [working with a fabrication shop](/resources/working-with-a-fabrication-shop/) explains what to send so the quote and the fit are right the first time.

## Washington requirements

Washington requires contractors to be registered, bonded and insured, regulates electrical work at the state level in many areas, and applies sales tax to most construction work based on the job site. We confirm how a given project will be handled before we quote, and we put it in writing.

## Start with the details

Use the [quote form](/quote/) and include the town. If it is a fit, we will say so and set up a visit. If it is not, we will tell you quickly and, where we can, point you to someone closer.
