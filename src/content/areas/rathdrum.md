## Designing for open ground

Rathdrum sits where the prairie meets the mountain. Most of the gate work here is on acreage: five, ten and twenty acre parcels with a gravel or paved drive coming off a county road. The land is generous and the weather is not.

**Wind is the first design load.** A gate leaf is a sail on a hinge. On sheltered sites that rarely matters. On the prairie it decides the infill, the frame and the operator. We favor open infill, braced leaves and positive latching so a gust cannot rack the gate.

**Snow is the second.** Drifts form on the downwind side of fences, berms and buildings. If a gate sits in that zone, its swing arc fills. We look at prevailing wind and what is upwind before choosing the gate's location and type.

## What gets built here

- **Double swing ranch gates** in heavy tube, with rails and a diagonal brace.
- **Cantilever slides** for wide openings and for owners who want privacy.
- **Entry arches** with the family or ranch name cut from plate.
- **Solar operators** where the meter is a long way off.

The drawing on this page is a concept ranch gate between stone pillars.

## Around the lakes

Twin Lakes and Spirit Lake bring a different pattern: wooded lots, cabins and seasonal use. Gates there are smaller, and remote access for a caretaker or a guest matters more than width.

## Planning notes

- **Road approach.** Most rural roads here belong to a highway district. Anything at the edge of the road goes through them.
- **Aquifer area.** Much of the prairie lies over the regional aquifer. That affects some kinds of site work, though rarely a gate. Ask the county if you are doing wider improvements.
- **Stock.** If animals are behind the gate, tell us. Infill, latching and fail-safe behavior change.
