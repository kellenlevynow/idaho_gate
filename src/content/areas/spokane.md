## One region, two states

People live in Post Falls and work in Spokane Valley. Businesses run yards on both sides. From our side of the line, Liberty Lake is closer than Sandpoint. Serving Spokane County is a matter of doing the same work under a different rulebook.

## Residential work

**City neighborhoods.** On the South Hill and in older parts of the city, lots have alleys, sidewalks and mature trees. Gates tend to be lower and closer to the house, and privacy from the street is a common goal. The drawing on this page is a concept privacy gate with sheet panels and an open band at eye level.

**Spokane Valley and Liberty Lake.** Newer homes, wider lots and frequent HOA review. Modern slat and flat-top gates suit much of the housing.

**The edges.** Green Bluff, Mead, Newman Lake and Otis Orchards have acreage, orchards and longer drives. Wide swing and sliding gates, some on solar.

## Commercial and industrial

The Spokane metro has the region's largest concentration of commercial property. Typical needs are cantilever slide gates for yards and storage, access control, enclosures, bollards and code-compliant railings. See [commercial gates](/gates/commercial-and-security-gates/) and [enclosures and guards](/fabrication/enclosures-and-guards/).

## Washington specifics to know

- **Contractor registration.** Verify any contractor with the Department of Labor and Industries.
- **Electrical.** Washington regulates electrical work and permits at the state level in many areas. Power for an operator is done by a licensed electrician.
- **Permits.** City or county, depending on the address.
- **Fire access.** Gates across fire apparatus access roads need the fire code official's approval.
- **Sales tax.** Shown on the quote.

Our guide to [permits, HOA rules and fire access](/resources/permits-hoa-and-fire-access/) covers the questions to ask on either side of the line.
