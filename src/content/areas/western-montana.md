## Over the divide

The Idaho Panhandle is narrow. From Sandpoint, Highway 200 follows the Clark Fork into Sanders County. From Bonners Ferry, Highway 2 follows the Kootenai to Troy and Libby. From the Silver Valley, the interstate crosses Lookout Pass. The country on the far side looks like home: timber, river bottom, ranches and long gravel drives.

We take work there on a project basis. We do not claim routine service, and we will not quote a job we cannot support properly.

## What makes sense

**Ranch entrances.** Wide gates, entry arches and cut signs. The drawing on this page is a concept double ranch gate between pillars. These are substantial, one-time projects that justify the trip.

**Solar automation.** Many properties are far from utility power. A solar operator on a balanced gate works well when sized for winter. See [solar gate operators](/automation/solar-gate-operators/).

**Fabricate and deliver.** Gates, arches, panels and brackets built to your dimensions and hauled over for a local crew to install.

## How a Montana project runs

1. **Details first.** Photos, the opening width, the town, and what you want it to do.
2. **A straight answer.** Whether we can take it on and in what form.
3. **Measure.** One site visit, or dimensions from you or your contractor.
4. **Drawing and quote.** You approve both before anything is cut.
5. **Fabricate.** Through the winter if needed.
6. **Deliver and install** in the building season.

## Montana requirements

Montana has its own contractor registration through the Department of Labor and Industry, and its own permitting. Montana has no general sales tax, which changes how a quote reads compared with Washington. We confirm the specifics for your project and put them in writing.

## Winter planning

Design for the conditions you know: deep snow, hard frost, wind in the valleys. Footings go below local frost depth. Gates get ground clearance. Hardware is kept off the ground. Our guide to [gates in snow and ice](/resources/gates-in-snow-and-ice/) covers it.
