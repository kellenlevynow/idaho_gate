## Snow country design

Sandpoint sits on Lake Pend Oreille with the Selkirks behind it and a ski mountain above town. Winter arrives earlier and stays longer than it does in the south end of the Panhandle. For gates, that changes the rules.

- **Where does the snow go?** Plowed snow has to be stored somewhere that is not the gate's path, the operator or the keypad.
- **How does the plow get through?** The gate has to open fully and stay open for it. A hold-open switch is worth having.
- **What is on the ground?** Tracks, stops and low sensors get buried. We keep as much as possible off the ground.
- **What freezes?** Hinges, rollers and locks need the right lubricant and drainage so meltwater does not refreeze in them.

The drawing on this page is a concept cantilever slide gate, a design that suits these conditions.

## Typical properties

**In town and along the lake.** Smaller lots, some with design guidelines, many with views to protect. Lower gates, pedestrian gates and privacy screens are common.

**Sagle and the Selle Valley.** Acreage and small farms. Wide gates, often manual or solar.

**Mountain and ski properties.** Steep paved drives, seasonal occupancy and guests who do not know the property. Access control that is easy for visitors matters.

## Planning notes

Sandpoint, Ponderay, Dover and Kootenai each have their own city rules, and most of the surrounding land is under Bonner County. Fire districts differ by area. We confirm requirements for your address during quoting.
