## Fitting metal to a mountain valley

East of Fourth of July Pass, the interstate follows the South Fork of the Coeur d'Alene River through a string of mining towns. The valley is narrow. Lots are small, old and often terraced into the hillside. Recreation has brought new owners and new building to the area.

The metalwork that matters most here is practical.

## Railings and stairs

The drawing on this page is a concept stair railing. Hillside homes have exterior stairs, landings, decks and retaining walls, and many have rails that are loose, low or missing. We build guards and handrails to current code, fitted to the as-built geometry, and finished for de-icing salt and snow. See [railings and handrails](/fabrication/railings-and-handrails/).

## Gates that suit the ground

- **Pedestrian and courtyard gates** often make more sense than vehicle gates on small lots.
- **Sliding gates** work where there is a level run along a wall or fence.
- **Swing gates** need a level arc, which is rare on valley-side drives.
- **Larger properties** up the side drainages have room for conventional driveway and ranch gates.

We will tell you which of those your site allows before you spend anything.

## Snow

Snow load is high here and it stays. Exterior metalwork should shed water, drain, and stand clear of where roofs dump. Gates need clearance and a place for plowed snow. Our guide to [gates in snow and ice](/resources/gates-in-snow-and-ice/) applies in full.

## Historic character

Wallace and parts of Kellogg have buildings worth respecting. For storefronts, balconies and fences on older buildings, we match traditional profiles and proportions where the design calls for it, and check with the city about any review that applies.

## Resort and commercial work

Ski and trail traffic supports lodging, restaurants and shops along the valley. We build entrances, signs, railings and [architectural metalwork](/fabrication/architectural-metalwork/) for those properties.

Shoshone County is in our extended service area and scheduled by route. Site visits are free and followed by a drawing and a written quote.
