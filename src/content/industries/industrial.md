## Access for heavy traffic

Truck yards, plants, utilities and storage sites need wide openings and gates that run all day. That almost always means a cantilever slide gate with a heavy bottom rail and an operator rated for continuous duty. Access is usually by credential for staff and vehicles, with a call box for visitors and an approved means of entry for the fire department.

Details are on our [commercial and security gates](/gates/commercial-and-security-gates/) page.

## Protecting equipment and people

The drawing on this page shows two simple things that prevent expensive days: a two-rail guard and a row of embedded bollards.

- **Bollards** keep vehicles off meters, panels, hydrants, columns and gate operators.
- **Guard rail** protects walls, racking and walkways along a travel path.
- **Machine guards** keep hands and clothing out of moving parts.
- **Cages and partitions** secure tools, stock and hazardous areas.
- **Barriers and gates** separate pedestrian routes from vehicle routes.

We build these to fit the actual equipment and the way maintenance gets done, because a guard that is a nuisance comes off.

## Platforms, stairs and supports

Mezzanine stairs, crossover platforms, equipment stands, pipe supports and skids are routine fabrication. Where they carry people or significant load, an engineer's design governs and we build to it. Guards and handrails on industrial stairs and platforms follow the code or regulation your site is under.

## Repair and modification

Plants change. A conveyor moves and a support has to follow. A loading area needs a wider opening. A guard needs a hinged panel for a new service point. We modify existing steel in place or pull it to the shop, and we match the original construction where it was sound.

## Finish and environment

Industrial steel is usually galvanized, or primed and painted to a site standard. Safety colors go where your program puts them. Tell us about chemical, washdown or abrasion exposure so the finish suits it.
